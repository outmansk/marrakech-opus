**In short:** yes. As a general rule, foreigners can buy a home in Morocco outright without being resident. A purchase goes through a preliminary agreement and then a final deed at the notary, and the sale is registered at the Land Registry when the property is titled. The key points: check the land title, bring the funds in through a bank, and keep the records.

## Can a foreigner buy property in Morocco?

Yes. Moroccan law generally allows foreigners to buy an apartment, a villa, a riad or commercial premises outright, with the same rights as a Moroccan buyer and no residence permit required.

The main exception is **agricultural land** outside urban areas: foreigners’ ownership of it is regulated and usually requires a certificate of non-agricultural use. If you are looking at a large plot on the outskirts of Marrakech, have a notary check this before signing anything.

## The steps of a purchase

1. **Search and viewings.** Set your budget, area and property type. If you live abroad, a first video viewing helps you shortlist before travelling.
2. **Checks.** Before you commit, your notary requests the land title certificate and reviews the property’s situation (see below).
3. **The preliminary sale agreement.** It sets the price, conditions and signing date, and usually comes with a deposit.
4. **The final deed.** It is signed at the notary (or before adouls). The balance of the price is paid at this stage.
5. **Registration.** The notary registers the deed and records it at the Land Registry (Conservation foncière): this entry is what makes you the official owner of a titled property.

## Essential checks

- **The land title.** A registered property has a land title whose certificate shows the owner, the surface area and any mortgage or easement. Unregistered property (held under a “melkia”) is riskier: read our guide [Land title, melkia, off-plan](/en/blog/land-title-melkia-off-plan-morocco).
- **Permits.** For a house or villa, ask for the building permit and the certificate of occupancy, and check that the buildings match the plans.
- **Co-ownership.** For an apartment, ask for the residence rules, the service charges and the state of the management accounts.
- **Outstanding debts.** The notary checks that there are no debts attached to the property.

## Funding the purchase from abroad

If you are not resident, transfer the funds by bank, in foreign currency, to an account opened in Morocco (banks offer accounts designed for non-residents). **Keep every transfer record**: they are required to repatriate the capital when you sell. The exact rules are set by the Foreign Exchange Office and change over time, so your bank and notary will give you the current procedure.

A mortgage from a Moroccan bank is possible for some non-residents, subject to income and deposit requirements.

## Buying costs

On top of the price, budget for registration duties, Land Registry fees, notary fees and, where applicable, the agency fee. The amount depends on the price, the type of property and your situation: **ask your notary for a written breakdown before signing the preliminary agreement**. Be wary of “all-in” percentages found online.

## Tips for buying property in Morocco as a foreigner

1. **Rent before you buy.** A few months in the area you are considering, on a [long-term rental](/en/long-term-rental), tells you more than any number of viewings: traffic, noise, shops, the commute to school or work.
2. **Choose your own notary.** You are free to pick a notary who is not the seller’s. Ask them to explain each step and each document in a language you understand.
3. **Read the land title certificate yourself.** Check that the owner, the surface area and the plot match what you are being sold, and that the certificate is recent.
4. **Never pay in cash.** Pay the deposit and the balance through the notary and by bank transfer, so that each payment is traceable.
5. **Ask for the costs in writing.** Before you sign the preliminary agreement, get a written breakdown of duties, fees and charges from the notary.
6. **Visit at different times of day.** On the outskirts of Marrakech, also check the access road, the water supply and the distance to the nearest shops.
7. **Be careful with off-plan.** For a property still to be built, check the guarantees offered by the developer: our guide [Land title, melkia, off-plan](/en/blog/land-title-melkia-off-plan-morocco) explains what to look for.
8. **Plan the resale from day one.** Keep the bank transfer records and every signed document: you will need them to repatriate the money when you sell.

## After the purchase

As an owner you will pay the local taxes attached to the home and, if you rent it out, you must declare the rental income. If you are considering holiday lets, check the required permits before buying.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can foreign nationals buy property in Morocco?

Yes. As a general rule, foreigners can buy an apartment, a villa or a riad in Morocco outright, with the same rights as a Moroccan buyer and no residence permit. The main exception is agricultural land outside urban areas, which follows specific rules: have a notary check each case.

### Can I buy a house in Morocco if I live abroad?

Yes. You do not need to live in Morocco or hold a residence card to buy a house, a villa or an apartment. You can view properties by video first, then sign at the notary in person or through someone holding a notarised power of attorney: ask your notary which option suits your case.

### What's the average cost of a house in Morocco?

We do not quote a national average: prices vary widely by city, area, size and condition, and we have no reliable source for a single figure. For Marrakech, our [villas](/en/for-sale/villas-marrakech) and [houses for sale](/en/for-sale/houses-marrakech) show their real asking prices.

### How much money do I need to retire in Morocco?

There is no single figure: it depends above all on your housing, health insurance and lifestyle. Housing is usually the biggest item, so start with real rents: our [long-term rentals](/en/long-term-rental) show current monthly prices, and our [moving guide](/en/blog/moving-to-marrakech-expat-guide) covers residency, banking and healthcare.

### Do I need to be resident to buy in Morocco?

No. Buying a home requires neither residency nor a residence card. If you fund the purchase from abroad, pay through a bank and keep the transfer records: they are needed to repatriate the capital when you sell.
