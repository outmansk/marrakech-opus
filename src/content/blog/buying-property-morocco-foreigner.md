**In short:** as a general rule, foreigners can buy a home in Morocco outright without being resident. A purchase goes through a preliminary agreement and then a final deed at the notary, and the sale is registered at the Land Registry when the property is titled. The key points: check the land title, bring the funds in through a bank, and keep the records.

## Can a foreigner buy property in Morocco?

Yes. Moroccan law generally allows foreigners to buy an apartment, a villa, a riad or commercial premises outright, with the same rights as a Moroccan buyer and no residence permit required.

The main exception is **agricultural land** outside urban areas: foreigners’ ownership of it is regulated and usually requires a certificate of non-agricultural use. If you are looking at a large plot on the outskirts of Marrakech, have a notary check this before signing anything.

## The steps of a purchase

1. **Search and viewings.** Set your budget, area and property type. If you live abroad, a first video viewing helps you shortlist before travelling.
2. **Checks.** Before you commit, your notary requests the land title certificate and reviews the property’s situation (see below).
3. **The preliminary sale agreement.** It sets the price, conditions and signing date, and usually comes with a deposit.
4. **The final deed.** It is signed at the notary (or before adouls). The balance of the price is paid at this stage.
5. **Registration.** The notary registers the deed and records it at the Land Registry (Conservation foncière): this entry is what makes you the official owner of a titled property.

## Essential checks

- **The land title.** A registered property has a land title whose certificate shows the owner, the surface area and any mortgage or easement. Unregistered property (held under a “melkia”) is riskier: read our guide [Land title, melkia, off-plan](/en/blog/land-title-melkia-off-plan-morocco).
- **Permits.** For a house or villa, ask for the building permit and the certificate of occupancy, and check that the buildings match the plans.
- **Co-ownership.** For an apartment, ask for the residence rules, the service charges and the state of the management accounts.
- **Outstanding debts.** The notary checks that there are no debts attached to the property.

## Funding the purchase from abroad

If you are not resident, transfer the funds by bank, in foreign currency, to an account opened in Morocco (banks offer accounts designed for non-residents). **Keep every transfer record**: they are required to repatriate the capital when you sell. The exact rules are set by the Foreign Exchange Office and change over time, so your bank and notary will give you the current procedure.

A mortgage from a Moroccan bank is possible for some non-residents, subject to income and deposit requirements.

## Buying costs

On top of the price, budget for registration duties, Land Registry fees, notary fees and, where applicable, the agency fee. The amount depends on the price, the type of property and your situation: **ask your notary for a written breakdown before signing the preliminary agreement**. Be wary of “all-in” percentages found online.

## After the purchase

As an owner you will pay the local taxes attached to the home and, if you rent it out, you must declare the rental income. If you are considering holiday lets, check the required permits before buying.
