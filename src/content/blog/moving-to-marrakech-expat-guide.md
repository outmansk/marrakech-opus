**In short:** many nationalities, including EU, UK and US citizens, can enter Morocco without a visa for a tourist stay of up to 90 days. To stay longer you need to apply for a residency card. The simplest start is to **rent long term**, which also gives you the proof of address needed for most formalities.

## 1. Staying: visa and residency card

Depending on your nationality, you can enter Morocco without a visa for a tourist stay of up to 90 days. Check your case with the Moroccan consulate in your country before leaving.

To settle long term, you apply for a **residency card** (carte d’immatriculation) with the police services where you live. The documents usually include your passport, photos, proof of accommodation (lease or title deed) and proof of income or activity. The exact list depends on your situation (employee, retiree, investor, spouse of a Moroccan): check with the authorities before preparing the file.

## 2. Housing: rent first, buy later

Even if you plan to buy, rent by the year first: you will get to know the areas day to day before committing. Our guide [Best areas to live in Marrakech](/en/blog/best-areas-to-live-marrakech) helps you choose, and you will find our [apartments](/en/long-term-rental/apartments-marrakech) and [villas for long-term rent](/en/long-term-rental/villas-marrakech).

The written lease is your first official document: it serves as proof of address for the residency card, the bank and schools.

## 3. Banking

A Moroccan bank account makes it easier to pay rent, bills and everyday purchases. Banks usually ask for your passport, proof of address and proof of income. Non-residents can open specific accounts; if you plan to buy, keep a record of every transfer from abroad (see our guide [Buying property in Morocco as a foreigner](/en/blog/buying-property-morocco-foreigner)).

## 4. Healthcare

Marrakech has private clinics and specialist doctors, many of whom speak French and some English. Most expats take out **international health insurance** or private local cover. If you are retired, check with your pension fund and health insurer what remains covered after you move.

## 5. Schools

Marrakech has several international schools: schools following the French curriculum, as well as schools offering British or American programmes. Places are limited in some year groups: contact schools several months before the start of the year, and factor the school run into your choice of home.

## 6. Getting around

In Gueliz and Hivernage you can live without a car, walking and using petits taxis. On the outskirts (Palmeraie, Ourika or Fez roads), a car becomes essential. Marrakech-Menara airport is close to the city and connected to many European cities.

## 7. Everyday climate

Summers are very hot and winters mild by day but cool at night. During viewings, check the air conditioning **and** the heating, as well as the insulation: that is what makes the difference in comfort all year round.
